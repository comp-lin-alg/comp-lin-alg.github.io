Errata for 2026/27¶

Here we’ll list all the updates to the course notes and computational exercises for Autumn 2026. An up-to-date version is in the public repository.

Chapter 2: Linear algebra preliminaries¶

  • In Linear algebra preliminaries, the exercise on solving \(Qx=b\) referred to solveQ; the function is cla_utils.exercises2.solve_Q().

  • In cla_utils/exercises1.py, the docstrings of rank1pert_inv and ABiC now document the return value and the xr, xi arguments, respectively.

  • In cla_utils/exercises2.py, the docstring of orthog_proj no longer mentions an input vector v, which is not an argument.

Chapter 3: QR factorisation¶

  • In QR factorisation, in the motivation of the classical Gram-Schmidt algorithm, the first column of \(Q\) is proportional to the first column of \(A\), and the third column (not row) of \(Q\) is the third column of \(A\) with the first two columns of \(Q\) projected out.

  • In QR factorisation, modified Gram-Schmidt transforms \(A\) to \(\hat{Q}\), which has orthonormal columns (and is unitary only if \(m=n\)); an arrow was missing in the displayed sequence of matrices.

  • In QR factorisation, in Householder triangularisation, the submatrix \(F\) is \((m-k+1)\times(m-k+1)\), rather than \((n-k+1)\times(n-k+1)\).

  • In QR factorisation, the stable sign choice in the Householder algorithm makes the target \(Fx=-\mbox{sign}(x_1)\|x\|e_1\) furthest from \(x\) (so that computing \(v\) involves no cancellation), rather than making \(v\) furthest from \(x\).

  • In QR factorisation, the slice \(A[0:3, 3:6]\) is a \(3\times 3\) submatrix, rather than \(4\times 4\).

  • In QR factorisation, the hint on Numpy’s inner and dot has been corrected: numpy.inner sums over the last index of each array (not over all indices), and neither function takes the complex conjugate, so \(x^*y\) should be computed with numpy.vdot or x.conj() @ y.

  • In QR factorisation, in the exercise on implicit multiplication, Householder is performed on the first \(n\) columns (not rows) of the extended array \(\hat{A}\).

  • In QR factorisation, the full \(m\times m\) matrix \(Q\) is obtained from \(Qe_1,\ldots,Qe_m\), rather than \(Qe_1,\ldots,Qe_n\) (which give only \(\hat{Q}\)).

  • In QR factorisation, in the least squares problem, \(\hat{Q}\) has orthonormal columns, and its column space equals that of \(A\) provided that \(A\) has full rank \(n\), which is assumed.

  • In cla_utils/exercises2.py, the docstring of GS_modified_R now refers to the upper (not lower) triangular formulation of modified Gram-Schmidt.

  • In cla_utils/exercises2.py, the docstring of GS_modified_get_R now describes the modified Gram-Schmidt step: \(R\) normalises column \(k\) and makes the later columns orthogonal to it (rather than making column \(k\) orthogonal to the previous ones).

  • In test/test_exercises2.py, test_GS_modified_R now also checks that \(Q\) has orthonormal columns and that \(R\) is upper triangular; previously a GS_modified_get_R returning the identity matrix would pass.

  • In cla_utils/exercises3.py, the docstring of householder_solve describes the returned columns as solution vectors, rather than right-hand side vectors.

Chapter 4: Analysing algorithms¶

  • In Analysing algorithms, in the operation count for modified Gram-Schmidt, the inner product is \(r_{ij}\gets q_i^*v_j\), rather than \(q_i^*v_i\).

  • In Analysing algorithms, modified Gram-Schmidt has \(n-i\) inner iterations over \(j\) for each \(i\), rather than \(n-i-1\).

  • In Analysing algorithms, the Frobenius norm is \(\|A\|_F=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^2}\); the absolute value was missing, which matters for complex matrices.

  • In Analysing algorithms, the absolute and relative condition numbers are defined with a limit over small perturbations, \(\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{0<\|\delta x\|\leq\delta}\), rather than a supremum over all \(\delta x\neq 0\), so that they measure the conditioning of \(f\) at \(x\).

  • In Analysing algorithms, in the example on the roots of \(x^2-2x+c\), the perturbed coefficient should be \(c=1-\delta c\) (rather than \(1+\delta c\)) for the roots to be \(1\pm\sqrt{\delta c}\).

  • In Analysing algorithms, machine epsilon \(\varepsilon=2^{-53}\) is half the distance between 1 and the next floating point number (which is \(2^{-52}\)), not the distance itself.

  • In Analysing algorithms, in the backward stability of the Householder algorithm, Householder transformations are reflections, not rotations.

  • In Analysing algorithms, the exercise on the stability of the Householder algorithm has been corrected: \(R_1\) is a random upper triangular matrix, and the exercise now states that \(A=Q_1R_1\) is formed and factorised as \(A=Q_2R_2\) before comparing.

  • In Analysing algorithms, each step of back substitution requires \(m-i\) multiplications and subtractions, rather than \(m-i-1\).

  • In Analysing algorithms, backward stability of computing \(\tilde{Q}^*b\) means that \((\tilde{Q}+\delta Q)\tilde{y}=b\) for some small \(\delta Q\); this does not follow from \(\tilde{y}=(\tilde{Q}+\delta Q)^*b\), as was stated.

  • In Analysing algorithms, in the proof that solving \(Ax=b\) via QR is backward stable, the result should read \(b=(A+\Delta A)\tilde{x}\); a bracket was misplaced.

Chapter 5: Finding eigenvalues of matrices¶

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, after the Schur factorisation theorem, the eigenvalues are read from the diagonal of \(T\), not from the diagonal of \(A\).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, the stopping criterion for the iterative transformation to Schur form should measure the strictly lower triangular part of \(A_k\), rather than the lower triangular part of \(A\).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, the argument for why the transformation to Schur form must be iterative should conclude that an explicit formula for the roots of the characteristic polynomial is impossible in general (not “explicit in general”).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, the reduction to upper Hessenberg form takes \(m-2\) steps, rather than \(m-1\).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the explanation of why a single similarity transformation cannot triangularise \(A\), the product should be \(Q_1^*(Q_1^*A)^*\) rather than \(Q_1^*(Q_1^*A)\), and it forms linear combinations of rows, not columns.

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the reduction to Hessenberg form, Householder transformations are reflections, not rotations.

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the Hessenberg pseudo-code, the normalisation should be \(v_k\gets v_k/\|v_k\|_2\), rather than \(v_k/\|v\|_2\).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the operation count for the reduction to Hessenberg form, the right multiplication costs \(\sim 2m^3\) FLOPs; \(\frac{10}{3}m^3\) is the total for the left and right multiplications together.

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the proof of convergence of power iteration, the eigenvector estimate follows from the eigenvector expansion of \(A^kv^0\), not from the Taylor series; the eigenvalue estimate then follows from the quadratic accuracy of the Rayleigh quotient.

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, the exercise on power iteration referred to A3 and B3; the functions are cla_utils.exercises9.get_A3() and cla_utils.exercises9.get_B3().

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, inverse iteration converges to the eigenvector for which \(1/|\lambda-\mu|\) is largest (not smallest), i.e. whose eigenvalue is closest to \(\mu\).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the convergence of Rayleigh quotient iteration, the eigenvalue error is \(|\lambda^{(k)}-\lambda_J|\), rather than \(|\lambda^{(k)}-q_J|\).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, the exercise on cla_utils.exercises9.rq_it() should ask for Rayleigh quotient iteration, rather than inverse iteration.

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the hint on complex QR factorisation, \(\mbox{sign}(x_1)\) should be replaced by \(e^{i\arg(x_1)}\), rather than \(-e^{i\arg(x_1)}\); the minus sign reintroduces the cancellation that the choice of sign is meant to avoid.

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, the convergence condition for simultaneous iteration with \(n<m\) vectors also requires \(|\lambda_n|>|\lambda_{n+1}|\).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the simultaneous iteration pseudo-code, \(\hat{Q}^{(0)}\) is an \(m\times n\) matrix with orthonormal columns (unitary only when \(n=m\)), and the QR factorisation computes \(\hat{Q}^{(k)},\hat{R}^{(k)}\).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the connection between the QR algorithm and inverse iteration, the indices should be \(m\) rather than \(n\) (\(e_m\), \(\tilde{r}^{(k)}_{mm}\)), and the last column of \({Q'}^{(k)}\) is the result of \(k\) steps of inverse iteration with \(\mu=0\) applied to \(A^T\), starting from \(e_m\).

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, in the description of shifts in the practical QR algorithm, it is the last diagonal entry of \(A^{(k)}\) (not of \(R^{(k)}\)) that converges, via inverse iteration, to the eigenvalue of \(A\) of smallest magnitude.

  • In Finding eigenvalues of matrices, the Rayleigh quotient shift computed from \(A^{(k)}\) is \(A^{(k)}_{mm}=(q_m^{(k)})^TAq_m^{(k)}\), and it is used as the shift \(\mu^{(k+1)}\) for the next iteration (rather than \(\mu^{(k)}\)).

  • In cla_utils/exercises8.py, hessenberg_ev used cla_utils.norm without importing cla_utils, so it (and hence ev and its test) failed with a NameError; it now uses numpy.linalg.norm.

  • In cla_utils/exercises9.py, the docstrings of inverse_it and rq_it have been corrected (rq_it had text copied from inverse_it, and its eigenvalue history has length maxit), and “factoration” is now “factorisation”.

Chapter 6: Krylov methods¶

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, the last column of the Krylov matrix \(K_n\) should be \(A^{n-1}b\), rather than \(A^{n_1}b\).

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, the orthogonal projection of \(Av\) onto the Krylov subspace is \(PAv=\hat{Q}_n\hat{Q}_n^*Av\); the \(A\) was missing on the right-hand side.

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in the exercise on recycling the QR factorisation in GMRES, the least squares matrix is \(\tilde{H}_k\) rather than \(H_k\) (and \(\tilde{H}_{k-1}\) rather than \(H_{k-1}\)). Householder transformations are reflections, not rotations.

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in the GMRES convergence bound, \(p_n(A)=Vp_n(\Lambda)V^{-1}\), rather than \(Vp_n(\Lambda^s)V^{-1}\).

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in “Knowing when to stop”, invertibility of \(A\) is not needed for the residual and pseudo-residual to tend to zero as \(x^k\to x^*\); it is needed for the converse, that a small residual implies a small error.

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in the bound on the relative error, the denominators should be \(\|x^*\|\) rather than \(\|x\|\).

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in “Knowing when to stop”, the relative error is bounded by the condition number of \(A\) (not of \(\|A\|\)) times the relative residual; if the condition number is large, a small residual can coexist with a large relative error (not a large condition number).

  • In cla_utils/exercises10.py, get_AA100, get_BB100 and get_CC100 now load their data files from the cla_utils directory; previously they only worked when run from inside that directory.

Chapter 7: Preconditioning Krylov methods¶

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the proof that a stationary method does not converge when \(\rho(C)\geq 1\), the eigenvalue condition \(|\lambda|>1\) should be \(|\lambda|\geq 1\), to include the case \(\rho(C)=1\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the proof that \(\rho(C)<1\) implies convergence, \(\lambda^k\) in the eigenvector expansion should be \(\lambda_i^k\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the proof that the eigenvalues of the symmetrically preconditioned matrix lie in \([1-c,1+c]\), the square root \(S\) should satisfy \(SS=M_s^{-1}\), rather than \(SS=M_s\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the SOR convergence condition \(0<\omega 2\) should be \(0<\omega<2\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the finite difference example, \(f_k=f(k/n)/n^2\) should be \(f_k=f(k/(n+1))/(n+1)^2\), consistent with the grid spacing \(h=1/(n+1)\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the example “Jacobi iteration for the example matrix” contained swapped spatial and eigenvector indices and used sine instead of cosine when combining exponentials. The corrected eigenvalues of \(D^{-1}A\) are \(\lambda_l=1-\cos(l\pi/(n+1))\), for \(l=1,\ldots,n\). Hence the maximum is \(1+\cos(\pi/(n+1))\), attained at \(l=n\), and the upper bound for the scaled Jacobi parameter is \(\omega<2/(1+\cos(\pi/(n+1)))\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the introduction to Chebyshev acceleration, the coefficients \(c_{jk}\) should be sought for \(j=0,\ldots,k\), including the coefficient of the initial guess \(x^0\). The eigenvalues to be made small are those of the matrix \(p_k(C)\), rather than of the polynomial \(p_k\) itself. After changing variables to the interval \([\alpha,\beta]\), the leading coefficient is \(2^{2k-1}/(\beta-\alpha)^k\), rather than \(2^{2k-1}/(\beta-\alpha)\). The accelerated iterate is \(y^k=\sum_{j=0}^kc_{jk}x^j\), rather than a sum with \(x^k\) in every term.

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the commented-out “Accelerated Jacobi’s method” block, the iteration matrix is \(-M^{-1}N=I-D^{-1}A\); the minus sign was missing. Also, if \(\lambda\) is an eigenvalue of \(I-D^{-1}A\), then \(1-\lambda\) is an eigenvalue of \(D^{-1}A\), rather than \(D^{-1}\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the symmetric convergence analysis should use \(\lambda_{\max}=\|A\|_2\), rather than \(\|A\|_2^2\). The positivity argument for \(\hat{\mu}_{\min}\) uses the square of the smallest singular value of \(M^{-1}A^{1/2}\), rather than its minimum eigenvalue.

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the CG preconditioner must be symmetric positive definite. The theorem placing the preconditioned eigenvalues in \([1-c,1+c]\) also assumes that \(A\) is symmetric.

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the eigenvalue clustering proof uses the relation \(Cv=(1-\lambda)v\), without assuming an eigendecomposition. Eigenvalue clustering alone does not guarantee fast GMRES convergence; for diagonalizable matrices, eigenvector conditioning also enters the convergence bound. The simplified proof of stationary convergence when \(\rho(C)<1\) covers the diagonalizable case.
